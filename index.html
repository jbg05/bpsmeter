01 / the report

One number, and every ticket behind it.

For each payout we find the cheapest venue that would have accepted that exact ticket size, at its published or quoted rate, and compare it with what you actually paid. The result is free, and it's yours to keep.

  • Per-fill repricingAgainst the best single venue, the best split across venues and a composite benchmark.
  • BreakdownsBy corridor, by provider and by month, including where your volume is concentrated.
  • Signed resultsFills where you beat the model count against the gap, not only the ones where you lost.
  • Nothing silently droppedA ticket we can't price is listed with the reason, next to what you paid for it.
  • Traceable to your filesWhat each file contributed, and every row we couldn't use, with its line number and why.
  • PDF and JSONA document for your finance team and the raw data for your engineers.

02 / the routing sheet

Then a sheet your desk can actually follow.

The findings become a standing instruction. For each corridor, a handful of ticket-size bands, each naming the venue to use (or the order to fill a split in) and the all-in cost to expect. Your desk places the trades. We never do.

Every ticket in your export is re-priced under the band it falls in, so the headline is what following the sheet would have done. Figures shown are from the sample book.

band tolerance
0.5 bps

Inside a band, the instruction is never more than 0.5 bps worse than deciding that exact ticket on its own. That's what keeps the sheet short enough to use.

your constraints
exclude or restrict

Leave out venues you won't onboard, whole channels or specific issuers, or limit the sheet to GENIUS Act permitted issuers. Excluded venues never appear.

03 / quotes

Priced on real quotes, capped at real liquidity.

Where a venue publishes prices, we pull them read-only at several ticket sizes and measure how much size it can actually take. No venue is ever offered past the size it could fill.

04 / your data

We see six columns. Nothing else.

The analysis needs date, corridor, provider, notional, fee and cost in bps. Our anonymizer runs on your machine first, strips everything else and tells you exactly what it removed.

your machine
$ node cli.mjs anonymize --fills payouts-q3.csv --out safe.csv

kept (safe to send)
  date, corridor, provider, notional_usd, fee_usd, bps

dropped (never leaves your machine)
  Status, Payment ID

# safe.csv is the only file you'd ever send

No custody, no credentials

We never hold funds, never ask for venue logins and never place a trade.

Nothing sent anywhere

The anonymizer and the report engine read a local file and write a local file. The only outbound calls are for public market quotes.

Used once

Your file is used for your report and deleted on request, under a mutual NDA and a data processing agreement. Or share your screen for fifteen minutes and send nothing.

05 / api

The same engine, before the ticket goes out.

Design partners get pre-trade routing over an API. Send a corridor, a size and a deadline, and get back the route, the expected all-in cost and the quotes behind it. It isn't public yet.

POST /v1/routedesign partner preview
curl https://api.bpsmeter.com/v1/route \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $BPSMETER_KEY" \
  -d '{"corridor":"USD-COP","notional":250000,"deadline":"2m"}'
